1. A bhavaṅga that flows by in a passive state when one of the five physical sense organs comes in contact with its object (atīta bhavaṅga).

2. A bhavaṅga that vibrates for one thought moment (bhavaṅga calana).

3. A bhavaṅga that cuts off the flow (bhavaṅga upaccheda).

4. A citta that turns towards the object through the sense door that has been stimulated (pañcadvāravajjana).

5. The appropriate sense consciousness; in the case of the eye, for example, eye consciousness (cakkhu viññāṇa).

6. Next a thought moment — the sampaṭicchana citta — which has the function of receiving the object.

7. When the object has been received another thought moment, called the santīrana citta, arises performing the function of investigating the object.

8. The object having been investigated, a thought moment called the votthapana citta occurs, determining the nature of the object. This stage has been described as 'the gateway to a moral or immoral thought process'. The thought-moments 1-8 are all ahetuka and either kiriya or vipāka. That is, they have no ethical roots and are either purely functional or resultants.

9 to 15. The object having been determined, the most important stage from an ethical standpoint

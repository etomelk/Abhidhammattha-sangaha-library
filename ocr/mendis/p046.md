(b) Expiration of the life span (*āyukkhaya*). Life in different planes of existence has its own maximum duration. When this maximum is reached, death occurs even if the reproductive kammic force is not exhausted. Any reproductive kamma left unexpended will re-materialise a new life in the same plane.

(c) Simultaneous exhaustion of the reproductive kammic energy and the expiration of the life span (*ubhayakkhaya*).

(d) The interference of a stronger opposing kamma (*upacchedaka kamma*) which obstructs the flow of the reproductive kamma causing death before the life term expires. This cause accounts for sudden 'untimely' deaths, seen especially in children.

The first three causes are responsible for 'timely' deaths (*kāla maraṇa*), the fourth for 'untimely' deaths (*ākāla maraṇa*). The four may be illustrated by the extinguishing of an oil lamp, which may be due to any of four causes: exhaustion of the wick, exhaustion of the oil, simultaneous exhaustion of both wick and oil, or some extraneous cause like a gust of wind.

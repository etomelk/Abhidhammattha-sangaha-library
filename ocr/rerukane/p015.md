to meet the Tathāgata. The Tathāgata briefly delivered in a few words, what was taught each day as if showing the great ocean by stretching out the arm while standing on the shore. The *Arahant* with four special powers was able to comprehend the Dhamma which was delivered in brief. The *Abhidhamma* he learnt from the Buddha was delivered in somewhat more detail so that his five hundred pupils could understand. Those five hundred Bhikkhus became learned in *Abhidhamma* at the end of the discourse. ***Abhidhamma Piṭaka* found today is the *Abhidhamma* taught by the general of Dhamma the Sāriputta *Mahā thero***. Although it was taught by the Sāriputta *Mahā thero*, it is considered a teaching of the Buddha as it was taught according to the method and ideas of the Tathāgata.

Although some say that there was no *Abhidhamma Piṭaka* during the first Dhamma council, the fact that three *Piṭaka* were chanted at the first council is shown as-

> ***“Upāliṃ vinayaṃ pucchi - suttantānanda paṇditaṃ***
>
> ***piṭākaṃ tīṇi saṅgītiṃ - akaṅsu jinasāvakā”***

in the Pañcasatika khandhaka which belongs to the Cullavagga Pāḷi in the Vinaya Piṭaka.

If there was no *Abhidhamma Piṭaka* at that time, there should have been another *Piṭaka*, so that three *Piṭaka* could have been chanted. As there is no other *Piṭaka* than the *Sutta Piṭaka, Vinaya Piṭaka* and *Abhidhamma Piṭaka; Abhidhamma Piṭaka* should have been there, along with the *Sutta* and *Vinaya*
